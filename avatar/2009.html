Hello, there! Today is a Saturday day in 2009.
I registered my first social media account online. QQ was the major instant messaging platform in China at the time. It was on its way to becoming one of those “mega apps” that allowed you to access virtually all forms of online entertainment: blogging, music streaming, video gaming, etc.
QQ’s primary function was similar to MSN; however, QQ came with a much flashier profile template that allowed users to create an avatar that represented their online presence:
As a 10-year-old, I wasn’t particularly well-versed in the virtual world that QQ had to offer. My avatar was one of the default pixel-drawn girlies with a middle-parted bob haircut. She wore a white tank top and a grey mini skirt—also a default outfit set that came with registration.
I was envious of the older children of my family friends. The older kids knew how to purchase QQ credit gift cards with their real pocket money at the newsstands. The QQ credit, which had real monetary value, allowed them to dress up their avatars in fancy clothes, animated hairstyles, and all sorts of sparkly digital accessories.
My mother did not understand what I was asking for when I told her I wanted a QQ credit gift card at the newsstand. She did not have a QQ account until 2013, and she certainly didn’t approve of the idea of spending her hard-earned money on virtual clothing. . .
